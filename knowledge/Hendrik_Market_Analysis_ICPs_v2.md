# Market analysis — lead ICPs (for Hendrik)
For each ICP: who they are, the signal (why now), their day-to-day situation, the pain, what Zyphe does, and the layer underneath the benefit (what it means to the person). Then a 4-part sequence each: a first email plus three pain-point emails, with a call after email 2.

## How we're prioritizing
Two different questions were getting mixed together, so I split them:
- **Outbound engine (lowest-hanging, where Hendrik's sequences go):** Web3/crypto, BNPL (CCD2), and foreign banks entering the US/EU. Each has a real reference and a trackable signal, so the message writes itself.
- **Marquee lane (enterprise motion, not volume outbound):** the big regulated banks and the stadium play. Huge deals but 6-12 month cycles, so we don't measure the outbound test on them.
- **Events-led:** adult / age-restricted. Highest urgency and whitespace, but it's a closed, trust-based sector that buys from names it meets at events, so we enter through XBIZ / Creator ConX, not cold sequences.

**Why Web3 leads:** it's where every one of our references already is (Supra, Protocol Labs, Yescoin), the no-honeypot story is native to the buyer, and the cycle is fast.

Zyphe proof points to draw from (use only what fits): agents that **finish** L1/L2 KYC/KYB/AML end to end, each decision with an audit-ready rationale, only ambiguous cases reaching a human; **Compliance-as-a-Service that takes on defined regulatory liability for the operated scope (no other IDV vendor does this)**; no central PII store (sharded, encrypted, user-held vault); **KYC Passport** (verify once, reuse, one-click re-verification); 190+ countries; document checks in about 15 seconds; typically 20-30% lower per-verification; SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR; live over existing systems in weeks including shadow mode; 5-minute sandbox, public pricing, free tier.

## ICP 1 — Web3 / crypto (exchanges, L1s, wallets, consumer apps)
*References we already have: Supra, Protocol Labs, Yescoin. This is our proven ground.*

**Signal (why now):** MiCA is fully applicable to crypto-asset service providers (registration plus AML/KYC), the Travel Rule applies, and specific triggers are trackable: a token or exchange launch, expansion into a new region, a compliance/AML hire, a competitor breach, or crossing a user-growth milestone.

**Their actual situation:** a crypto product scaling globally and fast, that now has to run KYC/AML across many countries, for users who are privacy-native and distrust handing their ID to a centralized vendor. A breach at their verification vendor would torch community trust overnight, and their compliance team is tiny next to the user volume.

**Pain:** they have to verify users across 190+ countries and satisfy MiCA/AML without building a honeypot their community won't forgive, and without hiring a compliance team the business can't justify.

**The layer underneath (what it means to them):** they scale onboarding worldwide without becoming the breach headline that ends community trust. Privacy turns into a trust edge instead of a cost. The founder or ops lead adds compliance capacity without a headcount they'd have to defend to the board, and verify-once-reuse means users stop rage-quitting at a second ID upload.

## Other lanes (not the outbound engine, but where real revenue sits)
**Adult / age-restricted (events-led).** Highest urgency of anything on the list (EU DSA proceedings, UK OSA enforcement with fines started, France's ARCOM double-anonymity standard) and the whitespace is wide because incumbents are honeypots getting fined. But it's a closed, trust-based sector that buys from names it meets at events. Enter through the XBIZ / Creator ConX seminars and workshops we're already building, plus warm outreach, not cold sequences.

**Stadiums / sports (marquee bench).** The Juventus playbook: a unique wedge (nominative tickets, restricted live facial recognition, our no-stored-biometric route) and a marquee reference. Few accounts and long, bespoke cycles, so it's a logo-and-reference play, not a volume engine.

## Worth testing next (ICPs we're not currently naming)
- **PSPs / EMIs / payment institutions** — Verification of Payee is now mandatory in SEPA and PSD3 is coming; high-volume merchant KYB and a clear "why now."
- **Marketplaces / gig / sharing economy** — seller and worker KYB/KYC at volume, plus DAC7 and fraud pressure; the agentic-capacity story lands.
- **Dating / social / trust and safety** — identity plus age plus deepfake and romance-scam pressure, and OSA exposure; a brand-safety buyer.

## A note on "banks due to DORA"
Worth being honest with ourselves: DORA is an ICT operational-resilience and third-party-risk rule, not a KYC/AML driver, so it doesn't create demand for what we sell. It's a good secondary line inside a banking conversation ("our no-honeypot architecture also lowers your third-party ICT and concentration risk under DORA"), but not an ICP to build a sequence around.
