# Zyphe marketing and growth strategy
Prepared by Edoardo Mustarelli · Confidential

## How to read this
This is a working strategy, not a wish list. Each proposal is laid out the same way so we can decide fast and hold ourselves accountable: the idea, the problem it solves, why I am proposing it, the evidence and numbers behind it, what success, a neutral result, and failure look like, how we measure it and against what, and exactly how to implement it including software, cost, and expected ROI where it can be estimated from reputable sources. Sources are linked throughout.

The frame: with cold calling as our primary motion, a team of two or three, and a three-month push to close and raise, marketing's job right now is not to be a standalone demand engine. Its job is to make the calls and the outbound land, and to start building the inbound that pays off in the second half. Everything below is prioritized against that.

## Principles I am holding us to
These are the non-negotiables that sit under every proposal:
- **Protect the sending domain.** Bad lists destroy deliverability and can blacklist zyphe.com, which is very hard to reverse. We validate every address, cut non-commercial contacts, and route cold volume through separate warmed domains so the primary domain stays clean. This is not optional after the recent complaint.
- **One clean list, kept clean.** A single validated list in HubSpot, deduped, with an unsubscribe on every sequence.
- **CRM discipline feeds the investor story.** Stale deals closed with a reason, every live deal carries an amount, a segment (SMB / Mid-Market / Enterprise), and a dated next step, because our reports filter by segment.
- **Nothing reads like a cold blast.** Reference a real trigger or prior contact, name one specific pain, offer a remedy.
- **One source of truth for numbers.** We lock a single metric set and use it in every asset, so a prospect who checks two of our things never sees a mismatch.

## Executive summary: the proposals
Ranked by leverage now.

Tier 1 (this month, cheap, high trust-per-hour):
1. Founder-led LinkedIn across all four of us, with internal amplification.
2. G2 and Capterra reviews for social proof.
3. A high-value newsletter, published to LinkedIn and emailed to a cleaned list.
4. Reddit and community presence.

Tier 2 (start now, pays off in 4-8 weeks):
5. Guest podcast appearances.
6. Instagram, set up and modeled on Sumsub.
7. A content engine (Pressmaster plus atomization) under all of the above.

Tier 3 (queue for bandwidth or a hire):
8. Regulatory-deadline webinars.
9. An original research report.
10. A partner and referral program.

Cross-cutting: trigger-based ABM (funding rounds, regulatory deadlines, competitor news) that feeds the cold-call and outbound list.

The rest of this document is the deep dive on each, and it ends with a measurement dashboard, key facts, and a to-do list.

# Tier 1

## 1. Founder-led LinkedIn, all four voices, with amplification
**Idea.** Each of the four of us (Michelangelo, Hendrik, Manuel, me) posts 2-3 quality posts a week to build a genuine personal reputation in identity, compliance, fintech and AI, not as a corporate mouthpiece. We comment on and reshare each other in the first hour of every post.

**Problem.** We are effectively invisible to buyers who vet vendors before they ever take a call, and our company page has no pull. When we cold-call a US prospect, nothing about us has pre-warmed them.

**Why.** Founder-led is now the dominant B2B distribution motion; personal accounts beat company handles. It is free, it compounds, and it is the cheapest way to make the calls land.

**Evidence.** 92% of B2B buyers will engage with a salesperson who is a known thought leader, and 47% of decision-makers say thought leadership influences their buying decisions (Edelman/LinkedIn) ([MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/articles/2026/54564/b2b-advocacy-marketing-growth-strategy)). Employee amplification can extend organic reach by up to 200% and delivers far more reach than corporate posting, and employee-shared content gets roughly 8x the engagement of company content ([GaggleAMP](https://www.gaggleamp.com/employee-advocacy-statistics-you-need-to-know), [Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/employee-advocacy-b2b-marketing-game-changer/)). Carousels are the top-performing format at ~46% engagement, native video under 90 seconds with a hook in the first 3 seconds wins, and comments in the first 60 minutes drive the algorithm ([getviews](https://getviews.ai/blog/complete-linkedin-growth-guide-2026), [connectsafely](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/linkedin-post-ideas-engagement-2026), [Workflows.io](https://www.workflows.io/blog/founder-led-marketing)).

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success in 90 days: each person posting consistently, follower growth and a measurable rise in profile-sourced inbound conversations. Neutral: posting but flat engagement, which means the hooks or the topic focus are off. Failure: under one post a week and no engagement lift, which means we stop and rethink.

**How we measure, against what.** Weekly: posts shipped per person (target 2-3), engagement rate per post (benchmark carousels ~46%, text far lower), follower growth, and profile-sourced meetings booked. Baseline is roughly zero today, so any consistent inbound is upside. Tool for analytics: AuthoredUp and Taplio dashboards.

**How to implement.** Each person picks one narrow topic to own for 90 days (mine: age verification and DSA; Michelangelo: agentic compliance and liability; Manuel: the privacy architecture). Write with **AuthoredUp** (~$19/mo, formatting, hooks, analytics) and manage the queue and idea library with **Taplio** (~$39-65/mo). Draft with Claude, generate carousels and clips with Pressmaster. Internal amplification is a shared Slack channel where new posts get dropped for comments within the hour. **Cost:** ~$60-85/mo plus time. **ROI:** not precisely attributable, but this is the channel the 2026 data says drives B2B inbound; we attribute via profile-sourced meetings and inbound mentions.

## 2. G2 and Capterra reviews
**Idea.** Get our happy clients (Protocol Labs, Filecoin, Twotixx, Bondex, Karpatkey, ETHDenver) to leave reviews, then put the badges on the site and in cold emails.

**Problem.** A skeptical buyer, especially in regulated segments, will not take a call with a vendor that has no third-party proof, and our competitors have review presence.

**Why.** Reviews are the fastest, cheapest trust asset we can create, and trust is exactly what converts a cold touch into a meeting. It also supports the "become a known name" goal.

**Evidence.** Trust signals dominate B2B buying: referrals and peer proof account for a large majority of closed revenue and convert far better than cold sources (referral leads convert ~70% better, and referrals drive 50-70% of closed revenue) ([GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/b2b-referral-marketing-statistics/), [Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/referral-program-statistics)). Reviews are the public, scalable version of that peer proof.

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: 5-8 published reviews in two weeks and a visible G2 presence. Neutral: 1-2. Failure: 0, which means the ask process is broken and we fix it.

**How we measure.** Reviews published, star rating, and a soft read on cold-email reply lift once the badge is added.

**How to implement.** Template the ask, make it a 5-minute favor, offer to draft a starting point for the client. **Cost:** free (G2 basic). **ROI:** high and cheap; one extra meeting a month from added credibility pays for the whole effort many times over.

## 3. Newsletter, LinkedIn plus email
**Idea.** One high-value issue, published as a LinkedIn Newsletter for free network reach and emailed to our cleaned HubSpot list. Weekly if sustainable, otherwise monthly. Format: the one regulation that matters, an honest take, one useful link.

**Problem.** We have no owned audience and no consistent nurture asset, so warm contacts go cold and cold-call follow-ups have nothing to offer beyond "let me send a deck."

**Why.** A newsletter is an owned channel that compounds, doubles as the follow-up asset, and positions us as an intelligence source rather than a vendor.

**Evidence.** Owned nurture is where warm pipeline is kept alive; the same thought-leadership dynamics apply (47% of decision-makers influenced by thought-leadership content, per Edelman/LinkedIn). On platform economics, Beehiiv lets us keep 100% of any monetization and gives real growth tooling, where Substack takes 10% and is just a writing tool ([Beehiiv vs Substack](https://whop.com/blog/beehiiv-vs-substack/)).

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: open rate above 30%, click rate above 2%, and a handful of replies or meetings per issue. Neutral: 15-30% open. Failure: below 15% open, which signals a list or deliverability problem to fix before scaling.

**How we measure, against what.** Open rate (B2B benchmark 20-40%), CTR (benchmark ~2-3%), replies, and meetings sourced. Never send from an unvalidated list.

**How to implement.** Build and validate the list first (**NeverBounce/ZeroBounce**, ~$20-50), publish on **Beehiiv** (free to ~$40/mo) and cross-post as a LinkedIn Newsletter. Draft with Claude from our knowledge base so the metric set is consistent. **Cost:** ~$20-90/mo. **ROI:** reactivation of dormant pipeline; measured by list-sourced meetings.

## 4. Reddit and community presence
**Idea.** Show up in r/fintech, r/compliance and the active LinkedIn and Slack compliance groups, contribute value, never pitch, and become the person tagged on eIDAS, DORA, and age-verification questions.

**Problem.** The communities where compliance and identity buyers actually live are not served well by any vendor, and we are absent.

**Why.** It is free, it compounds, and it is where the age-verification and DSA conversations (our adult and XBIZ push) are happening in real time.

**Evidence.** Community and peer channels are where B2B trust forms; peer influence drives the majority of closed revenue (see referral evidence above). Our own experience confirms the direction: signal-based, human, value-first outreach converts where blasts do not.

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: recognized contributor status and a few inbound conversations a quarter. Neutral: posting without traction. Failure: it reads as marketing and gets ignored, which means we change the approach.

**How we measure.** Karma/engagement on helpful posts, inbound DMs, and mentions. Monitor keywords with **F5Bot** or **Syften** (free/low cost) so we never miss a relevant thread.

**How to implement.** One genuinely useful post a month plus fast, helpful replies. **Cost:** ~$0-10/mo. **ROI:** slow compounder, low cost, keep it light but consistent.

# Tier 2

## 5. Guest podcast appearances
**Idea.** Get Michelangelo (and later me) onto established fintech, compliance and RegTech podcasts as a guest.

**Problem.** Many decision-makers listen but do not read, and we have no audio presence.

**Why.** It is borrowed authority at near-zero cost and the fastest path to a trusting audience, and each appearance atomizes into clips and a newsletter item.

**Evidence.** Thought-leadership formats influence buying (47% of decision-makers, Edelman/LinkedIn), and long-form audio is the highest-trust version of it.

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: 1-2 booked appearances a month and measurable content spin-off. Neutral: pitching without bookings. Failure: no traction after a month of outreach, which means the angles need work.

**How we measure.** Appearances booked, downloads/reach where shared, and clips produced.

**How to implement.** Build a target list of 10-15 shows, pitch with ready angles (who holds the liability when an AI agent runs compliance, the honeypot problem, the 2026 age-verification wave). **Cost:** time only. **ROI:** high per hour; attribute via referral traffic and mentions.

## 6. Instagram, modeled on Sumsub
**Idea.** Set up an Instagram (we do not have one) and run educational carousels and clips, matching Sumsub's cadence, not their budget.

**Problem.** A visible gap versus the category leader on a channel where they do well.

**Why.** Cheap presence that supports brand recall and gives cold calls a more credible footprint.

**Evidence.** Sumsub is the benchmark in KYB/KYC social presence; visual formats (carousels, short video) are the top performers across platforms in 2026 ([getviews](https://getviews.ai/blog/complete-linkedin-growth-guide-2026)).

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: a consistent feed and modest follower growth. Neutral: posting without growth. Failure: it eats time with no return, which means we throttle it back to Pressmaster-automated only.

**How we measure.** Posting consistency, follower growth, reach.

**How to implement.** Schedule via **Buffer** or **SocialPilot** (~$20-30/mo), content from Pressmaster. **Cost:** ~$20-30/mo. **ROI:** low direct, supports brand; keep the time budget small.

## 7. Content engine (Pressmaster plus atomization)
**Idea.** One insight becomes a week of LinkedIn posts for the four voices, a newsletter issue, a Reddit answer, and an Instagram carousel, using Pressmaster plus Claude/Gemini, wired to our knowledge base so it stays on-message.

**Problem.** A two-person marketing effort cannot produce enough content by hand to keep four voices plus a newsletter plus social alive.

**Why.** Atomization is the only way the volume is feasible for our team size, and it keeps the metric set and positioning consistent.

**Evidence.** Content atomization is the standard multiplier behind founder-led programs; one recording or insight routinely becomes 8-10 assets.

**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: the engine reliably fills the calendar with on-brand content each week. Neutral: it produces content that needs heavy editing. Failure: output is off-brand or thin, which means we fix the knowledge base it draws from.

**How we measure.** Assets produced per input, time saved, and on-brand pass rate.

**How to implement.** Pressmaster (via Hendrik's agency account) plus Claude/Gemini, scheduled through Taplio and Buffer. **Cost:** Pressmaster via Hendrik. **ROI:** measured in team hours saved and content volume enabled.

# Tier 3 (queue)

## 8. Regulatory-deadline webinars
**Idea.** One webinar per deadline (age verification/DSA, CCD2, MiCA), co-hosted with a law firm or analyst, gated recording, 3-step nurture.
**Problem/Why.** Deadlines create urgency and pre-qualified demand; a credible co-host doubles reach. **Evidence:** the DSA/OSA/ARCOM enforcement wave is live and creates real buying pressure ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/you-must-be-tall-click-online-safety-act-and-age-appropriate-access)). **Success:** N registrants and M SQLs per webinar; treat low performers as free activations and stop. **Measure:** registrants, attendance, SQLs, pipeline. **Implement:** webinar tool plus a co-host; measure like a pilot and only renew what converts. **ROI:** a single asset can generate leads for 12 months.

## 9. Original research report
**Idea.** A "State of Identity / Best KYC Provider 2026" report: survey 100-200 compliance pros on LinkedIn for free, publish a ~10-page PDF, launch as a webinar, pitch to Finextra and Biometric Update.
**Problem/Why.** We have no journalist-grade owned asset; original data earns press and positions us as an intelligence source. **Success:** downloads, press pickups, and SQLs. **Measure:** downloads, media mentions, pipeline sourced. **Implement:** LinkedIn survey plus a landing page ("get your free copy," short format). **ROI:** high-leverage, 12-month shelf life; real effort, so queue it.

## 10. Partner and referral program
**Idea.** A lightweight referral kit and channel: activate Innowise, recruit integrators (Alloy, Oscilar, Sardine, Unit21, Flagright) and consultancies, and give partners a simple 2-page kit.
**Problem/Why.** Two or three people cannot reach the market alone; partners multiply reach without headcount. **Evidence:** B2B referral programs convert ~70% better and return 3-5x ROI, and referrals drive 50-70% of closed revenue ([GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/b2b-referral-marketing-statistics/), [Shno](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/referral-program-statistics)). **Success:** 2+ active partners sourcing pipeline in 90 days. **Failure:** 0. **Measure:** partner-sourced pipeline and closed revenue, partner-sourced %. **Implement:** a referral kit plus outreach; near-zero cost. **ROI:** among the highest in B2B (3-5x cited).

# Cross-cutting: trigger-based ABM feeding outbound
**Idea.** Build the cold-call and outbound list off three real triggers, a funding round (60-90 day buying window), a regulatory deadline, and competitor news (a breach or a dropped vendor), rather than scattershot lists.
**Problem.** Generic blasts do not convert (our own Smartlead run produced roughly one positive reply from ~15,000 emails), and the 2026 environment punishes them.
**Why.** Targeted, triggered outreach is dramatically more efficient and is what actually books meetings.
**Evidence.** ABM raises average deal size by 171% and shortens sales cycles by 40% (Demandbase, widely cited) ([iCumulus](https://icumulus.ai/b2b/account-based-marketing/46-important-account-based-marketing-statistics-for-modern-marketers/), [Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/account-based-marketing-statistics/)). Small, tight lists reply at 5.8% versus 2.1% for large sends, and 2-3 follow-ups produce up to 42% of all replies ([Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/whats-the-expected-reply-rate-for-a-well-run-outbound-cold-email-campaign), [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)). Deliverability now decides everything: Google and Microsoft tightened bulk-sender rules in early 2026 and shared-IP tools dropped 30-50% ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-software/)).
**Success / neutral / failure.** Success: reply rate above 5% and meetings booked from triggered lists; a measurably higher win rate and shorter cycle on triggered versus non-triggered deals. Neutral: 3-5% reply. Failure: below 3%, which means something is broken (list, deliverability, or copy).
**How we measure, against what.** Reply rate (benchmark 3.43% average, >5% good), meetings booked (cold benchmark 0.1-0.5%), and win rate and cycle length on triggered versus non-triggered deals (target: move toward the ABM 171%/40% direction).
**How to implement.** **Clay** (~$150+/mo) for signal enrichment and research, **Amplemarket** as the brain, **Smartlead** (~$39/mo) for volume on separate warmed domains, email validation (~$20-50), and a parallel dialer (**Nooks** or **Orum**, custom pricing) for the daily US call block. Draft with Claude from the knowledge base. **Cost:** ~$250-400/mo plus the dialer. **ROI:** the highest-leverage spend we have, because it directly creates the pipeline the revenue plan depends on.

# Key facts to remember
- Email alone does not convert at our current setup (~1 reply per 15,000). The fix is triggered, tight, well-delivered outreach plus calls, not more volume.
- Deliverability is now the gating metric; separate warmed domains and a validated list are mandatory.
- Founder-led LinkedIn and partners are the two channels with the strongest evidence for inbound and revenue, and both are cheap.
- Small, personalized lists reply at ~3x the rate of large blasts, and follow-ups two and three capture ~42% of replies.
